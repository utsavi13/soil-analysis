🧪 Understanding Soil Parameters

pH Level

What it is: Measure of soil acidity or alkalinity (0-14 scale)

Ideal Range: 5.5 - 7.5 for most crops

Why it matters: Affects nutrient availability and microbial activity

💡 Tip: pH below 5.5 is acidic, above 7.5 is alkaline. Most crops prefer slightly acidic to neutral soil.

Nitrogen (N)

What it is: Essential for leaf and stem growth

Typical Range: 20-200 kg/hectare

Signs of Deficiency: Yellowing leaves, stunted growth

💡 Tip: Nitrogen is mobile in soil and needs regular replenishment.

Phosphorus (P)

What it is: Vital for root development and flowering

Typical Range: 10-100 kg/hectare

Signs of Deficiency: Purple leaves, poor root growth

💡 Tip: Phosphorus is less mobile and stays in soil longer.

Potassium (K)

What it is: Important for disease resistance and water regulation

Typical Range: 20-150 kg/hectare

Signs of Deficiency: Brown leaf edges, weak stems

💡 Tip: Potassium helps plants withstand stress and disease.

✅ Best Practices for Soil Health

1. Regular Testing

Test your soil at least once per growing season

  • Before planting
  • Mid-season for adjustments
  • After harvest for planning

2. Crop Rotation

Rotate crops to maintain soil health

  • Prevents nutrient depletion
  • Reduces pest buildup
  • Improves soil structure

3. Organic Matter

Add compost and organic materials

  • Improves water retention
  • Enhances nutrient availability
  • Promotes beneficial microbes

4. Balanced Fertilization

Apply fertilizers based on soil test results

  • Avoid over-application
  • Use right fertilizer at right time
  • Consider slow-release options

📊 How to Interpret Your Results

Understanding Suitability Scores

Our system calculates a suitability score for each crop based on your soil conditions:

  • Score 90-100: Excellent match - Highly recommended
  • Score 70-89: Good match - Suitable with minor adjustments
  • Score 50-69: Fair match - Requires fertilizer corrections
  • Score below 50: Poor match - Consider other crops

Nutrient Deviation Explained

Deviations show the difference between your soil and ideal requirements:

  • Negative (-20): Your soil has 20 kg/ha LESS than ideal - Need to add
  • Positive (+15): Your soil has 15 kg/ha MORE than ideal - Excess
  • Near Zero (±5): Your soil is close to ideal - Good condition

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How often should I test my soil?

A: At least once per growing season, ideally before planting. For intensive farming, test 2-3 times per year.

Q: Can I grow crops not recommended by the system?

A: Yes, but you'll need to make significant soil amendments. The system recommends crops that will perform best with your current soil conditions.

Q: How accurate are the fertilizer recommendations?

A: Our recommendations are based on standard agricultural guidelines. Always consult with local agricultural experts for final decisions.

Q: What if my pH is too low or too high?

A: For low pH (acidic), add lime. For high pH (alkaline), add sulfur or organic matter. Consult an agricultural expert for specific amounts.
